# Apex Credit Solutions Limited — Master Internal Compliance Policy (Unified Framework)

### Document Ref: ACSL/CP/MASTER/2026/V4 | Classification: Internal Use Only

---

## Section 1 — Purpose, Scope, and Governance Framework

### 1.1 Document Purpose

This Master Internal Compliance Policy establishes the baseline operational, risk management, and legal governance frameworks for Customer Due Diligence (CDD), Know Your Customer (KYC) protocols, Anti-Money Laundering (AML) controls, and Digital Lending Operations within Apex Credit Solutions Limited (hereinafter referred to as "the Company"). The foundational objective of this document is to institutionalize standard operating procedures that mitigate credit, legal, operational, and reputational risks. Furthermore, this policy serves as the core reference material for internal compliance audits and regulatory alignment assessments executed by the risk management team.

### 1.2 Regulatory Alignment

The provisions enshrined within this policy are drafted to ensure absolute alignment with the statutory mandates issued by the financial regulators of India. Specifically, this document incorporates the directives specified under the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Master Direction - Know Your Customer (KYC) Direction, 2016 (as updated dynamically), the RBI Guidelines on Digital Lending, the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002, and the Prevention of Money-Laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005. Any subsequent legislative modification, circular amendment, or notification issued by the central bank or the Financial Intelligence Unit - India (FIU-IND) shall automatically supersede the corresponding clauses contained herein until a formal revision is ratified.

### 1.3 Target Scope and Applicability

This policy applies universally, without exception, to all operational verticals, branch offices, central processing units, and digital delivery channels owned or operated by the Company. It imposes legally binding duties on all permanent employees, contractual staff, credit underwriters, customer relation executives, and the designated members of senior management. Additionally, the operational mandates defined in Section 4 extend structurally to all external Lending Service Providers (LSPs), Digital Lending Applications (DLAs), and outsourced collection agencies executing business activities under a formal corporate agreement with the Company.

### 1.4 Internal Governance and Accountability

The ultimate oversight for the enforcement of this policy rests with the Board of Directors of the Company, which shall delegate executive implementation authority to the Risk Management Committee (RMC). The Chief Compliance Officer (CCO) is designated as the principal custodian of this policy and is responsible for monitoring day-to-day corporate adherence, updating operational instructions, and conducting quarterly compliance reviews. The CCO shall function with complete structural independence and shall possess direct reporting access to the Audit Committee of the Board to ensure unhindered escalation of compliance anomalies.

## Section 2 — Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Core KYC Framework

### 2.1 Customer Identification Procedure (CIP)

The Company shall verify the identity of every prospective customer before establishing an account-based relationship, initiating credit underwriting, or executing any financial transaction. For individual customers, identity verification shall be completed utilizing Officially Valid Documents (OVDs), which are restricted to Passport, Driving Licence, proof of possession of an Aadhaar number, Voter's Identity Card, NREGA job card, or a formal letter issued by the National Population Register. The Company shall ensure that the collection of Aadhaar details remains entirely voluntary, and no applicant shall be denied credit services solely due to the non-submission of an Aadhaar number. For non-individual entities, identification shall require the submission of statutory registration certificates, tax registration records, and structural resolution proofs as detailed under the non-individual customer frameworks.

### 2.2 Beneficial Ownership Identification

For all corporate, partnership, and trust entities seeking credit facilities, the Company shall proactively identify the natural persons who ultimately exert control or possess a substantial economic stake in the customer entity. The specific quantitative ownership threshold that triggers mandatory Beneficial Owner (BO) identification for corporate entities is established at a strict limit of 15% or more of the shares or capital or profits of the juridical person. For partnership firms and unincorporated associations of individuals, the identification threshold is similarly fixed at 15% of the capital or profits of the entity. In the case of trust customers, the Company shall identify the settler, the trustees, the beneficiaries with a 15% or greater interest, and any other natural person exercising ultimate effective control over the trust structure.

### 2.3 CDD Measures for Non-Individual Customers

When onboarding corporate customers, the Company shall mandatorily collect the registered corporate name, proof of the legal form of the entity, the complete physical address of the registered office, and the operational addresses of all principal branches. The credit operations team shall verify these details through independent registries, including the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) portal and official tax databases. Furthermore, the Company shall collect the full names, updated residential addresses, and officially verified contact details of all active directors, partners, or trustees, alongside the specific identities of the senior management personnel holding executive decision-making power (such as the Chief Executive Officer or Managing Director). The legal capacity of the entity to enter into credit obligations must be established via a certified copy of the Board Resolution, Power of Attorney, or constitutive documents (Memorandum and Articles of Association) authorizing specific personnel to transact on its behalf.

### 2.4 Sanctions List Screening Process

To prevent the inadvertent onboarding of restricted entities, the Company shall maintain an automated, real-time screening engine integrated directly into the core customer origination system. The screening framework shall cross-reference all prospective customer names, directors, beneficial owners, and authorized signatories against the updated United Nations Security Council (UNSC) sanctions lists, the dynamic Consolidated List of Individuals and Entities maintained under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA), 1967, and global anti-money laundering watchlists. This sanctions dataset must be updated via automated technological scripts immediately upon the publication of any revision by the respective monitoring authorities. Furthermore, the Company shall execute a mandatory retrospective batch-screening of the entire existing customer database at a minimum frequency of once every 24 hours to capture any newly listed entities or overnight regulatory updates.

### 2.5 Video-Based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP)

The Company shall offer a consent-based Video-Based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP) as an approved alternative method for remote digital onboarding of individual retail customers. The V-CIP workflow must be executed exclusively by a fully trained, certified compliance official of the Company through a secure, live audio-visual application that utilizes robust end-to-end cryptographic encryption protocols. The application must programmatically capture the precise real-time geographic coordinates (geo-tagging) of the customer, ensuring the applicant is physically located within the sovereign borders of India during the session. The validity period of any OVD document utilized during the V-CIP interaction, including the clear capturing of the physical PAN card or e-PAN details, is strictly limited to the absolute currency of the underlying document, meaning expired documents shall trigger an immediate automated rejection of the V-CIP session.

### 2.6 Risk Categorization of Customers

All customers onboarded by the Company shall be classified into three distinct risk tiers—Low Risk, Medium Risk, or High Risk—based on a programmatic risk-scoring model executed at the time of account creation. The risk categorization parameters shall systematically evaluate multidimensional factors, including the customer’s geographic location, demographic profile, nature of business activity, source of income, corporate transparency, and historical credit behavior. High Risk designations shall be automatically applied to specific segments, including Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs), non-resident clients, trusts, charities, cash-intensive businesses (such as bullion dealers or real estate brokers), and companies with complex layered structures. Low Risk classification shall be reserved for salaried individuals with highly transparent bank-verified cash flows, regulated public sector undertakings, and blue-chip listed corporate entities.

### 2.7 Periodic KYC Update Frequency

The Compliance Department shall ensure that all active customer profiles are subjected to regular periodic updates to maintain the absolute accuracy of the database. The mandatory operational frequency for refreshing customer identity records and financial data points is explicitly governed by the assigned risk category: at least once every 2 years for all High Risk customers, at least once every 8 years for Medium Risk customers, and at least once every 10 years for Low Risk customers. The core banking system shall automatically generate digital notices to the customer three months before the arrival of the periodic update deadline. If the customer fails to submit validated, fresh KYC documentation within a maximum period of 90 days from the initial notice, the Company shall enforce a partial freeze on the account, restricting further loan disbursements while permitting recovery operations.

### 2.8 Record Retention Period

The Company shall preserve all essential documentation, transaction logs, and customer due diligence records in an unalterable, highly secure digital repository. All KYC verification files, V-CIP audio-video recordings, physical document scans, and corporate structural certificates shall be retained for a mandatory minimum period of 5 years. This 5-year retention timeline shall commence from the exact date on which the underlying business relationship with the customer is formally terminated, closed, or when the final loan obligation is fully liquidated. All transaction records and ledger logs linked to the account must be similarly preserved for a minimum period of 5 years from the specific date of the transaction to facilitate immediate extraction and presentation upon formal demand by statutory investigators or the central bank.

## Section 3 — Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Transaction Monitoring

### 3.1 Transaction Monitoring Framework

The Company shall operate an automated Transaction Monitoring System (TMS) designed to track all cash flows, loan repayments, and prepayment activities across the entire asset portfolio. The TMS shall utilize predefined algorithmic rules to detect anomalous behavioral patterns, including sudden large-volume pre-closures from unknown bank accounts, structured circular transactions, or multiple rapid repayments falling just below reporting thresholds. Any transaction that triggers a systemic alert shall be routed immediately to the AML Operations desk for independent manual analysis. The operations team must complete the preliminary investigation of every flagged transaction alert within a maximum period of 7 business days from the initial systemic flag.

### 3.2 Suspicious Transaction Reporting (STR)

Where the AML Operations desk finds prima facie evidence of tax evasion, illegal sourcing of funds, or structures lacking clear economic rationale, the file shall be escalated to the Principal Officer. If the Principal Officer confirms the suspicion, the Company shall draft a formal Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) detailing the behavioral metrics and financial pathways involved. In absolute compliance with statutory timelines, the STR must be electronically submitted to the Director, Financial Intelligence Unit - India (FIU-IND) within a maximum period of 7 days from the exact date on which the Principal Officer arrives at a conclusion of suspicion. The Company shall strictly enforce internal "tipping-off" prohibitions, ensuring that no employee discloses to the customer that an STR is being prepared, reviewed, or transmitted to government agencies.

## Section 4 — Digital Lending Operations and Partner Governance

### 4.1 Digital Lending Partnership Framework

The Company shall engage in digital loan origination, customer acquisition, and collection operations through third-party platforms only after establishing a formal corporate alliance with incorporated Lending Service Providers (LSPs). Prior to entering into a definitive agreement, the Risk and Compliance teams shall perform an exhaustive, mandatory due diligence process on the prospective LSP partner. This due diligence assessment shall comprehensively evaluate the LSP’s financial solvency, corporate governance standard, data privacy infrastructure, technological encryption architecture, and technical resilience against cyber threats. The Company shall ensure that all approved LSPs and their corresponding Digital Lending Applications (DLAs) are formally registered, updated, and published prominently on the public website of the Company for customer validation.

### 4.2 Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) Arrangements

The Company may selectively utilize Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) arrangements with its onboarded LSP partners to manage credit portfolio risks, provided such structures align with the Board-approved credit risk strategy. The cumulative DLG cover accepted by the Company across any specific, outsourced digital loan portfolio pool shall be subjected to a strict statutory ceiling capped at a maximum of 5% of the total disbursed loan principal amount within that specific portfolio. Every DLG contract must be explicitly framed as a legally enforceable agreement backed solely by permissible security formats, which are strictly limited to cash deposits, fixed deposits held under lien with a scheduled commercial bank, or irrevocable bank guarantees. The internal risk architecture shall systematically invoke the underlying DLG within 120 days of continuous borrower default, ensuring that individual loans are recognized as Non-Performing Assets (NPAs) based on standard prudential norms without altering the structural provisioning timeline. Furthermore, in accordance with accounting and Expected Credit Loss (ECL) alignment rules, the Company will dynamically recalculate its risk provisioning models whenever a guarantee layer is invoked or altered.

### 4.3 Loan Disbursal and Repayment Architecture

The Company shall maintain absolute control over the flow of funds across all digital lending channels to prevent the pooling of public money by unregulated tech intermediaries. All digital loan disbursements shall be executed directly from the core bank account of the Company into the verified, primary bank account of the specific borrower, completely bypassing any bridge, escrow, or pass-through account owned by the partner LSP. Similarly, all collection and repayment workflows must route funds directly from the borrower's personal account into the designated collection systems of the Company. LSPs are completely restricted from handling cash collections or operating settlement pools, except when recovering defaulted loans under explicit written authorization, where funds must be credited to the account of the Company on the exact same business day.

### 4.4 Key Fact Statement (KFS) Disclosures

Before the formal execution of any digital loan contract or the initialization of fund disbursement, the Company shall ensure that the borrower is presented with a standardized, clear, and unalterable **Key Fact Statement (KFS)** within the digital application interface. The KFS must display all critical financial vectors, explicitly detailing the exact loan principal amount, the nominal annualized interest rate, the finalized Annual Percentage Rate (APR) inclusive of all embedded processing fees, insurance premiums, and administrative costs, alongside specific calculation rules for late-payment interest penalties. The borrower must actively acknowledge the terms of the KFS via an IT Act-compliant digital signature or secure two-factor authentication string. A copy of the finalized KFS, the underlying loan agreement, and the detailed repayment schedule must be automatically transmitted to the borrower's registered email address and mobile number via automated system scripts immediately upon sanction.

### 4.5 Grievance Redressal Mechanism for Digital Loans

The Company shall maintain a dedicated, independent grievance redressal infrastructure tasked with tracking, investigating, and resolving disputes originating from digital lending applications and LSP operations. A designated Nodal Grievance Redressal Officer (NGRO) shall be appointed to monitor this channel, and their direct contact coordinates, email addresses, and toll-free helpline numbers must be published prominently across all active DLAs. The Company guarantees a strict, maximum Turnaround Time (TAT) commitment of 21 calendar days from the initial log entry to fully resolve and close any digital loan customer complaint. If a dispute remains unresolved past this 21-day window or if the customer remains dissatisfied with the internal decision, the digital platform must automatically display a direct link to the RBI Complaint Management System (CMS) portal to provide alternative regulatory recourse.

### 4.6 Data Privacy, Consent, and Minimization

All data acquisition, processing, and storage operations executed through the Company’s digital lending systems shall adhere strictly to data minimization principles. Digital lending applications representing the Company are completely barred from capturing omnibus or blanket permissions; instead, explicit, separate, and highly granular consent prompts must be generated for each distinct operational capability. Mobile applications are structurally prohibited from accessing the borrower's smartphone storage, media galleries, contact lists, or personal call logs, except for a one-time geo-location capture required exclusively to fulfill the regulatory V-CIP onboarding workflow. Borrowers retain the absolute legal right to revoke their data consent, deny third-party sharing, and demand the complete permanent deletion of all historically collected personal attributes from the servers of both the LSP and the Company once the underlying credit facility is closed in full.

## Section 5 — Audit, Reporting, and Regulatory Escalation

### 5.1 Internal Compliance Audit Cadence

The Internal Audit Department of the Company shall conduct an independent compliance audit of all KYC registries, transaction monitoring workflows, and digital lending operations at a mandatory frequency of once every six months. These audits shall evaluate operational adherence to this policy, verify the quantitative maintenance of the 5% DLG cap, check the processing speed of systemic alerts, and audit V-CIP records for protocol breaches. The formalized audit reports, alongside specific time-bound remediation schedules, shall be submitted directly to the Audit Committee of the Board within 15 days of audit completion. The CCO shall ensure that all operational gaps highlighted during the audit are structurally resolved within a maximum period of 30 days from the publication of the report.

### 5.2 Regulatory Reporting Cadence

The Principal Officer and the Chief Compliance Officer shall ensure that all mandatory corporate data filings are submitted to the regulatory authorities without any operational delay. The Cash Transaction Reports (CTRs) for all accounts exceeding ten lakh rupees or its equivalent in foreign currency must be filed electronically with FIU-IND by the 15th day of the succeeding month. Counterfeit Currency Reports (CCRs) and Non-Profit Organisation Transaction Reports (NTRs) shall be filed within the same monthly schedule. In addition, all operational metrics concerning digital lending partnerships, active LSP corporate links, and portfolio delinquency metrics must be reported to the Reserve Bank of India through the official Centralised Information Management System (CIMS) portal on a quarterly basis.

## Section 6 — Policy Review, Maintenance, and Amendments

### 6.1 Annual Review Mandate

This Master Internal Compliance Policy shall be subject to a comprehensive, structural review by the Compliance Department at least once every financial year to evaluate its ongoing operational efficacy and technological adaptability. The annual review process shall incorporate the feedback gathered from internal audit reports, suggestions from the credit operations desk, and evolving market benchmarks in digital credit deployment. Any proposed modification or structural expansion of the policy must be documented clearly in an executive review note and presented to the Risk Management Committee before final validation.

### 6.2 Approval Architecture for Amendments

All modifications, rewrites, structural alterations, or removals of clauses within this policy document require the formal written approval and final ratification of the Board of Directors of the Company. No individual department, business head, or executive officer possesses the authority to unilaterally waive or alter any mandate enshrined herein. The formal approval date and version history ledger must be updated inside the core configuration file of the policy immediately upon the passage of the resolving Board resolution.

### 6.3 Interim Addendum Power

Notwithstanding the provisions of subsection 6.2, in the event of sudden external regulatory updates, central bank circular shifts, or emergency Master Direction modifications issued by the Reserve Bank of India, the Chief Compliance Officer is fully empowered to draft and issue immediate, interim operational addendums to this policy. These interim updates will instantly take effect across all functional systems, ensuring our codebases and front-end lending applications adjust dynamically to changing regulatory requirements. The CCO shall introduce these interim addendums to the Board of Directors for formal retrospective ratification during the next immediate quarterly board meeting.

## Section 7 — Appendix: Regulatory and Operational Definitions

* **Lending Service Provider (LSP):** An agent of a Regulated Entity who carries out one or more of a lender’s functions or processes (such as customer acquisition, underwriting support, pricing determination, data collection, or loan recovery) under valid outsourcing rules.
* **Digital Lending Application (DLA):** Mobile and web-based applications, standalone portals, or digital interfaces operated by a Regulated Entity or its partner LSPs that facilitate digital credit origination, loan servicing, and customer management.
* **Default Loss Guarantee (DLG):** A contractual risk-sharing arrangement where an LSP compensates the lender for default losses up to a pre-agreed percentage of the underlying loan portfolio pool.
* **Key Fact Statement (KFS):** A mandatory, standardized document summarizing the critical cost terms, cooling-off timelines, and operational conditions of a loan facility provided transparently to a borrower prior to credit execution.
* **Video-Based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP):** A method of customer identification by an official of the Regulated Entity by undertaking a seamless, secure, real-time audio-visual interaction with the customer.
* **Officially Valid Document (OVD):** The passport, the driving licence, proof of possession of Aadhaar number, the Voter's Identity Card issued by the Election Commission of India, the job card issued by NREGA duly signed by an officer of the State Government, and the letter issued by the National Population Register containing details of name and address.
* **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD):** Additional, rigorous customer due diligence measures applied to higher-risk accounts, including verification of sources of wealth, senior management sign-offs, and intensive transaction tracking.
